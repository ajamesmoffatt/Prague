Count Lützow’s tour: Prague in 1902

A walk through Prague following Count Lützow’s chapter “Walks in Prague”, from The Story of Prague (J. M. Dent, 1902). The drawings are by Nelly Erichsen, who illustrated the book. The book was published in 1902, at the height of the Austrian Empire, and since then the city has changed in some ways a lot. As such, Lützow’s own words are in italics; the “Now” notes are mine. There are three walks, each starting where he starts: Walk 1 and Walk 2 from the Old Town (the Powder Tower), Walk 3 from Charles Bridge. Stops are shown in a sensible walking order, not always the book’s exact order. Prices are adult prices; ≈ means approximate, so check before you go.

Walk 1: the Old Town and the Jewish quarter

Lützow’s usual starting-place is the Powder Tower. He takes the visitor through Celetná to the market-place, then to the Carolinum and Bethlehem, and into the old ghetto. I’ve reordered a few stops so the route flows one way and doesn’t double back.

1Powder Tower

Climb ≈190kč (verify)

Immediately opposite the Hybernská Ulice is the powder tower.

Now: Lützow says it was skilfully restored in 1883 by Joseph Mocker. You can still climb it today.

2The Carolinum

Exterior free

From the Carolinum we can either take the Zelezna Ulice (Iron Street), which leads us back to the town hall, or through the Ovocni Trh (fruit market), reach the Celetná Ulice, or return to the Graben, at the end of which we reach the powder tower.

Now: Next door is the Estates Theatre, where Don Giovanni premiered in 1787.

3Týn Church

Free to enter

The Tyn Church was of very modest origin.

Now: In his history chapters Lützow follows its preachers: Waldhauser, Milič and Rokycana, the Utraquist archbishop.

4Kinsky Palace

≈250kč (verify)

The Tyn Church and the Kinsky Palace, which have already been mentioned, are also situated in the market-place.

Now: In 1902 he noted its library, strong on the French Revolution (17,470 works). It now houses the National Gallery’s exhibitions.

5St Nicholas (Old Town Square)

Free when open (verify)

Immediately behind the town hall is the Renaissance church of St. Nicholas, which, by permission of the city, is now used for the services of the Russian community.

Now: Since 1920 it has been used by the Czechoslovak Hussite Church.

6Old Town Hall and the market-place

Tower & Town Hall 350kč / 280kč youth

The market-place, the forum of Prague, plays a great part in the story of the town.

Now: Lützow’s Hus and 1621 chapters are set here. Note the Hus Memorial (1915) and the Marian Column were not in Lützow’s square: the memorial came after his book, and the column was toppled in 1918 (a replica went up in 2020). The Town Hall’s east wing was destroyed in 1945.

7Jewish Town Hall

Exterior free; Jewish Museum 600kč / 400kč

Entering the Rabbi Street, we see at our left the Jewish town hall, perhaps one of the most picturesque buildings in Prague.

Now: He describes Josefov as the ancient ghetto that “still preserves its medieval character”. Today’s Josefov was largely rebuilt after the slum clearances of the 1890s–1900s.

8Old Jewish Cemetery & Old Synagogue

Combined 600kč / 400kč students

Rabbi Löwi Bezalel, who died in 1609, is buried in the old Jewish cemetery (his legend and friendship with Tycho Brahe are told in Chapter I).

Paraphrased from Chapter I.

Now: The Old-New Synagogue is a separate ticket from the Jewish Museum combined ticket.

9Bethlehem Chapel (site)

≈90kč / 50kč reduced (verify)

Here once stood the Bethlehem Chapel, the cradle of Church reform, and the modest dwelling of Hus.

Now: In 1902 the chapel was a site rather than a building. It was reconstructed in the 1950s.

Walk 2: the river, Vyšehrad and Karlov

Lützow calls the New Town and Vyšehrad less interesting than the Old Town, but still worth the walk. He follows the Vltava south to Vyšehrad, then on to Karlov. His own text gives two ways back: along the river to the theatre, or by Karlov church.

1Francis I statue and the river

Free

Continuing to follow the Vltava, we soon reach the equestrian statue of the Emperor Francis I., which stands in the middle of a small garden.

Now: Beside the Old Town Bridge Tower and the Klementinum.

2Rotunda of the Holy Cross

Exterior free

Near here, in the Karolina Svetla Street, is the interesting Romanesque Chapel of the Holy Cross, one of the three earliest ecclesiastical edifices of Prague.

Now: One of Prague’s surviving Romanesque rotundas.

3National Theatre (‘Bohemian theatre’)

Performances from roughly 500kč

…follow the Podskal Street, and then the Palacky Quay… and finally reach the Bohemian theatre.

Now: He is describing the return from Vyšehrad, so you can walk it in the opposite direction.

4Vyšehrad

Grounds free; casemates 190kč; basilica ≈100kč

It is almost certain that the earliest inhabited spot within the precincts of the present city of Prague was the hill on the right bank of the Vltava or Moldau, known as the Vysehrad (‘higher castle’ or Acropolis).

Now: Today’s Vyšehrad is a quiet park fortress, with the cemetery, the basilica and the casemates.

5Rotunda of St Martin

Exterior free

He illustrates the Romanesque Chapel of St. Martin among the oldest buildings of Prague.

Now: One of the three Romanesque rotundas he lists.

6Karlov Church

Check opening times

Charles built the Karlov and the Church and Monastery of Emaus.

Now: He ends this walk here, at the corner of the Horská and Karlova Streets.

Walk 3: Malá Strana and the Hradčany

For the left bank Lützow starts from the Bridge of Prague and works up through Malá Strana to the Castle and the Belvedere, then down through the Chotek Park and back across the river to the Rudolfinum and the Powder Tower. I’ve left out the Strahov detour, which he treats in an earlier chapter.

1Charles Bridge

Free

For his wanderings through the parts of Prague that are on the left bank of the river the traveller will start from the far-famed Bridge of Prague, for so it is still called, though its official designation is the Charles Bridge, and there are now many other bridges at Prague.

Now: The statue of St John of Nepomuk, and the view of the Hradčany, are both in his drawings. “The view of the Hradcany from the bridge is one of the most striking ones in Prague.”

2Lesser Town Bridge Tower

≈190kč; both towers ≈340kč

He illustrates the bridge tower of the Malá Strana.

Now: Climbable today for the view.

3St Nicholas, Malá Strana

≈200kč / ≈150kč (verify)

Milič’s sermons, preached in the Church of St. Nicholas in the Malá Strana, soon attracted general attention.

Now: The present Baroque church was built long after Milič’s day. Lützow’s footnote reminds readers that Prague has two St Nicholas churches.

4Hradčany square

Free

He treats the Hradčany as the town’s greatest sight.

Now: Start of the castle district.

5Prague Castle and St Vitus

Main Circuit 450kč / 300kč reduced

He describes St Vitus, the Castle halls and the old gates.

Now: His drawings include the east gate, the Wenceslas Chapel and the Hall of Vladislav.

6The Belvedere

Depends on the exhibition

Close to the Belvedere is a fountain, which has rightly been called ‘one of the finest Renaissance fountains north of the Alps.’

Now: His introduction also calls Ferdinand I’s Belvedere villa one of the finest Renaissance buildings in Northern Europe.

7Chotek Gardens

Free

From the Belvedere we descend the Hradcany Hill, and passing through the Chotek Park reach the Bruska Street and the suspension bridge.

Now: The suspension bridge of his day has long gone.

8Rudolfinum

Gallery tickets vary

Crossing this bridge, we reach the old town and the Rudolphinum, and soon find our way back to the powder tower.

Now: The end of the circuit, and a short walk to the Powder Tower.
